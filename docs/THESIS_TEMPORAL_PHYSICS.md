# A Mathematical Framework for Time, Causality, and Speculative Temporal Scenarios

## Abstract
This thesis framework connects special relativity, general relativity, quantum theory, thermodynamics, cosmology, and selected speculative spacetime proposals. It separates validated equations from mathematical extrapolations and narrative hypotheses. It does not establish practical past-directed time travel.

## Research questions
1. How do proper time and coordinate time differ along a worldline?
2. What causal restrictions follow from Lorentzian geometry?
3. Which spacetime solutions contain closed timelike curves, and what assumptions do they require?
4. How do quantum measurement, decoherence, and thermodynamic entropy relate to temporal records?
5. Which hypothetical mechanisms are mathematically expressible but lack empirical support?

## Method
Use analytical derivations first, dimensional analysis second, numerical experiments third, and comparison with authoritative literature throughout. Every result must state its metric signature, coordinate conventions, units, initial conditions, and validity domain.

## Established foundation
For an inertial clock moving at constant speed v relative to an inertial frame in flat spacetime, proper time over coordinate interval Δt is Δτ = Δt√(1−v²/c²). For a static observer outside an ideal Schwarzschild mass, dτ/dt = √(1−2GM/(rc²)). These are domain-limited statements, not engineering recipes for a time machine.

The Einstein field equations relate spacetime curvature to stress-energy: Gμν + Λgμν = (8πG/c⁴)Tμν. Solutions depend on matter content, geometry, boundary conditions, and global assumptions. A mathematical solution does not by itself prove physical realizability.

For a quantum state represented by density operator ρ, Tr(ρ)=1 and ρ is positive semidefinite. Purity Tr(ρ²) and von Neumann entropy −Tr(ρ ln ρ) characterize aspects of mixedness; neither implies controllable branching between histories.

## Speculative mechanisms
Traversable wormholes, engineered closed timelike curves, extra dimensions, and controllable timeline transitions are treated as speculative or fictional unless an explicit model and evidence support a stronger label. Energy conditions, stability, quantum backreaction, and formation mechanisms are central obstacles.

## Falsifiability and reporting
A claim of new physics must specify a measurable observable, a quantitative prediction, uncertainty bounds, a comparison baseline, and a possible result that would count against the claim. Simulated plots are never to be presented as observational evidence.

## Conclusion
Mathematics can explore temporal scenarios without validating their physical existence. The productive research objective is to derive constrained models, identify contradictions and assumptions, and produce reproducible tests—not to assume that a time-travel device is possible.
